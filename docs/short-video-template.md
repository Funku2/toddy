# The Edge Short: reusable template

The template for every Biological Edge short (YouTube Shorts, Reels, TikTok, Facebook Reels). Built from a teardown of our first red light sauna video and from what the creator of Bolt Motivation says about how his Shorts are made.

## 1. Where the rules come from

**From Bolt Motivation's creator (interview summaries):**

- His formula is **hook, tension, payoff**. The hook is the first three to five seconds. Tension is about 90% of the video. The payoff resolves it. "If there isn't a worthwhile payoff, there probably isn't a worthwhile story."
- He often **opens on the most dramatic moment**, then goes back to give context.
- About 95% of viewers arrive by scrolling, so the opening seconds are "the audition".
- For motivational videos he uses a second framework: **call out the audience, address their pain, end on a challenge.**
- Finishing touches are glowing captions, colour grading and layered sound effects.
- He tracks **average view duration** above everything else.
- Each Short takes him about 10 hours. Quality over volume.

**From general Shorts guidance (creator blogs, not official YouTube data):**

- Most reach is decided in the first three seconds. A common target is under 30% swiping away.
- Rough benchmarks: under about 60% average retention tends to stall, 70 to 90% tends to break out.
- 15 to 30 seconds is the commonly recommended length for this kind of video.
- A second hook around the midpoint and an ending that loops into the opening both help retention.

**His title pattern** (from his 20 most recent titles, 1.3 to 17.6 million views each): a short past-tense line that withholds the outcome, an ellipsis, two emojis. "She Was About To Cry…🥺💔", "He Waited 3 Years For This Opportunity…🥹🏀".

One difference to keep in mind: his videos tell true stories with real footage of real events. Ours are second-person motivation with generated footage plus Todd's real photos and clips, which is his "call out, pain, challenge" framework. Real footage of Todd is the closest thing we have to his advantage, so use it wherever possible.

## 2. Teardown of the first sauna video

| Area | What it does now | Verdict | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Opening shot** | A dark, almost still shot of a man lying in bed | Weakest shot is first. Nothing moves and it reads as grey. | Open on the strongest image: the fist clenching under the red lights |
| **Hook line** | "Nobody is coming to save you." | Strong line, generic to all motivation content | Keep the tone, name the viewer: "You're not tired because you're fifty." |
| **Pacing** | 6 shots in 25.6 seconds, 4.3 seconds each | Too slow. The eye settles and the thumb moves. | 12 to 14 shots, 1.5 to 2.5 seconds each |

## 3. The template

### Length

- **22 to 28 seconds** for motivation and product shorts.
- **40 to 60 seconds** only for a true story with a real turn in it.

### Beat sheet (25 seconds)

| Time | Beat | What happens | Shots |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2s | **Hook** | Strongest image in the video, already moving. Hook text on screen. First line calls out the viewer. | 1 to 2 |
| 2 to 7s | **Pain** | Three quick pictures of the problem, each matching one short sentence | 3 |
| 7 to 9s | **Turn** | The reframe. Music drops. This is the second hook. | 1 |
| 9 to 18s | **Ritual** | The thing we do about it, shown as action. Tension builds. | 4 to 5 |
| 18 to 23s | **Payoff** | Real footage of the real product or result | 2 to 3 |
| 23 to 25s | **Challenge** | One line that sends the viewer back to the opening | 1 |

### Script rules

- 60 to 75 words in total.
- Sentences of eight words or fewer. One idea each.
- Second person, present tense: "you", not "people".
- Line one names who the video is for, and can be understood with no context.
- The turn uses the pattern "It's not X. It's Y." and softens any health claim: "maybe", "might".
- The last line can be heard as the lead-in to the first line.
- No product names in the voiceover. Show the product; name it in the description.
- No promises of results, no disease claims, nothing about prescription treatments.

### Hook lines to rotate

1. "You're not tired because you're fifty."
2. "Nobody tells men this after forty."
3. "This is why you wake up exhausted."
4. "You don't have an age problem."
5. "Most men quit right here."

### Picture rules

- 12 to 14 shots. None longer than 2.5 seconds.
- Movement in every shot: the subject, the camera, or a slow push-in.
- First frame is bright and high contrast. Never open on a dark shot.
- One colour world per video (red light, dawn gold, cold blue), with the pain beats desaturated and the payoff saturated.
- At least two shots are real footage or photos of Todd's own gear.
- Faces stay out of frame or in shadow unless it is Todd.
- Each 4 second generated clip is used as two shots: the first half, then the second half with a 15% punch-in. Same cost, twice the cuts.

### Caption rules

- 1 to 3 words on screen at a time, timed to the voice.
- All capitals, heavy sans-serif, white with a black outline.
- About 5% of frame height, centred, sitting about three quarters of the way down the frame so it stays clear of the product.
- One key word per line in a highlight colour that contrasts with the footage: gold (`#FFC83D`) on red or dark scenes, ember orange (`#D9541E`) on cool or neutral ones.
- Never over the product or a face.
- Hook text for the first 2 seconds sits at the top third and is different from the spoken words.

### Voice rules

- One voice across every video so the channel is recognisable. The current brand voice is the Higgsfield preset "Fraser" on the ElevenLabs engine (`text2speech_v2`, variant `elevenlabs`). The default engine mispronounced "fifty" with this voice. Todd's own cloned voice can replace it later.
- Never trim pauses inside the speech with a volume threshold: it clips quiet consonants ("tired" became "hired"). Shorten a long pause by removing only its middle.
- Every narration is checked before the edit: run speech recognition on it with no script and compare word for word. Regenerate any line that does not match.
- 2.8 to 3.2 words a second, with pauses of a quarter second or less between sentences.
- Pain beats lower and slower. Everything after the turn firmer and faster.

### Sound rules

- Music: sparse and tense to the turn, full after it. The drop lands on the turn word.
- Narration is generated in two parts, before and after the turn, so the second part can be faster and louder.
- Sound effects: a low hit on frame one, a soft whoosh on every second cut, a riser for the two seconds before the turn, an impact on the payoff shot.
- Voice always clearly on top. Music about 14 dB under the voice.

### Ending and brand

- No end card. The last frame cuts or loops straight back to the first.
- Logo watermark in a top corner at about 60% opacity for the whole video.
- The call to action lives in the description and the pinned comment.

### Title and description

- Title: outcome withheld, ellipsis, two emojis. "He Thought It Was Just Age…😳🔥"
- Description line one: the product or the free guide, with the link.
- Description line two: "Some links are affiliate links."
- Switch on the platform's AI-generated label where it applies.

## 4. Production recipe

1. Write the script to the beat sheet. Check it against the script rules.
2. Pick the two real shots first (Todd's photos or clips). Build the rest around them.
3. Generate six or seven 4 second clips at 1080p vertical (about 64 Higgsfield credits each).
4. Generate the voice and a 30 second music track.
5. Edit: split clips into shots, cut on sentence breaks, add sound effects.
6. Captions: transcribe the final voice track, burn in to the caption rules.
7. Check: watch it with the sound off, then with the picture off. Both should make sense.
8. Post the same file to all four platforms.

Budget per video: about 450 to 500 Higgsfield credits.

## 5. Pre-post checklist

- [ ] First frame is the strongest image and is already moving
- [ ] The viewer is named in the first two seconds
- [ ] The turn lands by second 9
- [ ] No shot is longer than 2.5 seconds
- [ ] There is real footage of the real product
- [ ] Captions are readable on a phone at arm's length
- [ ] The last line loops into the first
- [ ] No end card, no dead time
- [ ] No health promise, no prescription product, affiliate line in the description

## 6. What to measure after posting

| Number | Where | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|
| Viewed versus swiped away | YouTube Studio, per Short | Whether the hook works. Aim for 70% or more viewed. |
| Average view duration | YouTube Studio | Whether the middle holds. Aim for 80% of length or more. |
| Rewatches (view duration above 100%) | YouTube Studio | Whether the loop works |
| Link clicks | Site analytics | Whether the product beat works |

Change one thing per video (hook line, opening shot, voice, length) so we learn which change moved the number. These targets are community rules of thumb, not official figures; our own best video becomes the benchmark once we have ten posted.
