Sauna is a regular part of my week, and it is the recovery habit I find easiest to keep. It is also surrounded by a lot of big claims, so it's worth being clear about what is known.

## The headline study

Most of what you hear about sauna and longevity traces back to research from Finland, where sauna is part of daily life. The best-known study followed about 2,300 middle-aged Finnish men for roughly twenty years and was published in *JAMA Internal Medicine* in 2015.

Men who used the sauna four to seven times a week had a markedly lower risk of dying from heart disease, and from any cause, than men who went once a week. Longer sessions were linked to better outcomes too.

## What that does and doesn't prove

This was an observational study. It shows that frequent sauna users lived longer. It can't prove the sauna was the reason.

Men who take a sauna most days may also be men with less stress, more free time and better health to begin with. The researchers adjusted for a lot of that, but no adjustment is perfect.

Here's how I read it:

- **Reasonably well supported:** regular sauna use is associated with better heart health, and short-term studies show it lowers blood pressure for a while afterwards and makes your heart work in a way that resembles light exercise.
- **Plausible but less certain:** benefits for mood, sleep and muscle soreness. Many people report them. The trials are small.
- **Not supported:** "detoxing", meaningful fat loss, or using a sauna in place of exercise. Sweat is mostly water.

## How to start

The Finnish studies involved traditional dry saunas at around 80°C, with sessions that typically lasted 15 to 20 minutes.

1. **Start short.** Five to ten minutes, and get out when you want to.
2. **Build up** over a few weeks towards 15 to 20 minutes.
3. **Drink water** before and after.
4. **Cool down gradually.** Sit for a few minutes before you shower.
5. **Aim for regular.** Two or three sessions a week you'll keep beats seven you won't.

## Who should be careful

Heat is a real stress on the body. Talk to your doctor first if you have a heart condition, low blood pressure, or you take medication that affects blood pressure or sweating. Don't combine sauna with alcohol, and get out if you feel dizzy or unwell.
