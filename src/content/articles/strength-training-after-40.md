Men start losing muscle in their forties, slowly at first and faster with each decade. You don't notice it year to year. You notice it when the stairs get harder or your back goes out lifting a suitcase.

The good news is that this is one of the most reversible parts of ageing. Muscle responds to training at any age. Public health guidelines in Canada and from the World Health Organization recommend strength work at least twice a week for exactly this reason.

You do not need a complicated programme. You need a small number of movements, done consistently, with the weight going up over time.

## The five movements

Every session is built from the same five patterns:

1. **Squat:** goblet squat, leg press or box squat
2. **Hinge:** Romanian deadlift or kettlebell swing
3. **Push:** push-up, dumbbell press or overhead press
4. **Pull:** dumbbell row, cable row or pull-down
5. **Carry:** pick up something heavy in each hand and walk

That's the whole list. Curls and the rest are optional extras.

## The week

Train three days a week with a rest day between, such as Monday, Wednesday and Friday.

- **Warm up** for five minutes: brisk walk, then one light set of each movement.
- **Do 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps** of each of the first four movements.
- **Finish** with 3 carries of about 30 seconds.
- **Rest** 90 seconds to two minutes between sets.

A session takes about 45 minutes.

## The one rule that makes it work

When you can do 12 clean reps on all three sets, add a little weight next time. That drops you back to 8 or 9 reps, and you build up again.

That slow climb is what builds muscle. Without it you are just exercising.

## What changes after forty

- **Recovery takes longer.** Three hard sessions beats five mediocre ones.
- **Joints want a warm-up.** Never skip it.
- **Leave a rep or two in the tank.** Grinding to failure on every set costs more than it pays.
- **Pain is information.** Swap the exercise for one in the same pattern that doesn't hurt.

## Food and sleep do half the work

Muscle is built from protein, and most men over forty don't eat enough of it. Research on older adults who train commonly points to somewhere around 1.2 to 1.6 grams per kilogram of body weight a day. For a 90 kg man that is roughly 110 to 145 grams. Get a decent serving at every meal and you are most of the way there.

Then sleep. Training is the signal; the building happens overnight.

## Before you start

If you have a heart condition, high blood pressure, or you haven't exercised in years, check in with your doctor first. Then start lighter than you think you need to. The goal for the first month is to finish every session wanting to come back.
