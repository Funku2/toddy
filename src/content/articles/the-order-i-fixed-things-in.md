For years I had the same three problems. I was anxious and stressed most of the time. My stomach was a mess. And I had no energy, the kind of tired that a weekend doesn't touch.

I treated them as three separate problems, and every so often I'd attack all of them at once with a new diet, a new gym membership and a new morning routine. It never lasted more than a couple of weeks.

What worked in the end was much less dramatic. I fixed one thing at a time, and the order turned out to matter.

## 1. My head came first

I had always assumed I'd feel calmer once I got healthy. It was the other way round. While I was running on stress, I couldn't keep any habit going, because every hard day ended with me dropping it.

So the first thing I worked on was my mental health. That was the foundation, and nothing else held until it was in place.

If you take one thing from this article, take this: if you are anxious and wound up all the time, start there. Talk to your doctor or a therapist. It is not a detour from getting healthy. It is the first step.

## 2. Then I started moving, gently

My first year of exercise was Pilates. That surprises people. It was the right call for where I was: unfit, stiff and not ready to load a barbell.

A year of it gave me a body that could move properly and, more importantly, the habit of showing up.

## 3. Then I got strong

After that year I moved to lifting weights, and that is where the visible change happened. I have put on a good amount of muscle since, at an age when most men are quietly losing it.

Strength training is the lever I'd now call non-negotiable for any man over forty. I've written a [minimum effective plan](/articles/strength-training-after-40/) if you want somewhere to start.

## 4. Then I looked at my hormones, with a doctor

Only after the basics were in place did I get proper bloodwork done and talk to a clinic about testosterone. I am now on testosterone replacement therapy, prescribed and monitored by a medical clinic.

I am glad I did it in that order. TRT is a medical treatment with real trade-offs, not a shortcut, and it is a decision for you and your doctor. If you are curious, start with [the bloodwork conversation](/articles/bloodwork-conversation-with-your-doctor/).

## 5. Recovery holds it all together

The last piece was recovery. I use the sauna regularly and I take a short list of supplements. None of that would have done much on its own. On top of everything else, it is what keeps me feeling good week to week.

## What I'd tell you at the start

- **Pick one lever.** Not five. The one that is hurting you most.
- **Give it ninety days** before you judge it.
- **Measure something,** so you can see progress on the days you can't feel it.

I'm 51 and I feel better than I have in years. It did not take a miracle. It took the right order and enough patience to let each step settle.
