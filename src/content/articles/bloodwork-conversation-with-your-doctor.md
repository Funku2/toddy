I lived with low energy for years. When I finally had proper bloodwork done and sat down with a clinic to go through it, I had real information to act on for the first time. In my case, part of what followed was testosterone replacement therapy, which a clinic prescribes and monitors for me.

That is my story, not a recommendation. The reason I'm telling it is that the bloodwork conversation was the turning point, and most men never have it properly.

## Why "I'm tired" doesn't work

Fatigue has dozens of possible causes: poor sleep, sleep apnoea, low iron, thyroid problems, blood sugar, depression, medication, low testosterone, or just not moving enough. A doctor with ten minutes and a vague complaint can't sort through all of that.

You get a much better appointment if you arrive with specifics.

## Before you go

Write down, for two weeks:

- How many hours you sleep, and whether you wake up rested
- When in the day your energy drops
- Changes in mood, motivation, sex drive or strength
- Whether your partner says you snore or stop breathing in your sleep
- Everything you take, including supplements

## What to ask about

These are common things to raise. Your doctor will decide what is appropriate for you.

- **Blood count and ferritin,** to look at anaemia and iron stores
- **Thyroid (TSH)**
- **Blood sugar (HbA1c or fasting glucose)**
- **Cholesterol panel**
- **Vitamin D,** particularly through a Canadian winter
- **Kidney and liver function**
- **Testosterone,** if your symptoms point that way. It is measured in the morning, and a low result is normally repeated before anyone acts on it.
- **Blood pressure,** which needs no blood at all. It is worth [checking at home](/shop/blood-pressure-monitor/) as well.
- **Prostate screening (PSA).** Ask whether and when it makes sense for you.

## Questions worth asking

- "Is this result normal for my age, or is it good?"
- "What would you want to see change by my next test?"
- "Could my sleep be the cause? Should I be assessed for sleep apnoea?"
- "What would you try first: lifestyle, or treatment?"

## A word on testosterone

Low testosterone is real and treatable. It is also heavily marketed. Treatment is a long-term medical decision with benefits and risks, including effects on fertility, and it needs ongoing monitoring. It belongs in a conversation with a doctor who has your full results in front of them, not in an online quiz.

Sleep, strength training, body weight and alcohol all affect testosterone as well, and they are worth getting right whatever you decide.

## Keep your results

Ask for a copy every time. One result tells you very little. Three results over two years show you which way you are heading.
